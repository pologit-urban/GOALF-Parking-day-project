01 — BACKGROUND

Why a golf course
on the street.

Park(ing) Day is about making people look.

In 2005 a design studio in San Francisco called Rebar paid for one metered parking space, rolled turf over it, put down a bench and a tree, and sat there for two hours. That was the whole thing. It worked because people walking past stopped and looked at a piece of street they had never really looked at before.

That is what Park(ing) Day is for. A parking space is the least used thing on a street. Most of the day it holds one car that is not going anywhere. The point is to get attention on that, and to show what the same few square metres could be if people were let in.

We did not run Park(ing) Day. We borrowed the idea and took it off the parking bay. What we used is street space in front of the Yeonsu-gu 2nd Office — the paved strip people walk past every day and nobody ever stops on. We wanted them to walk over and try something rather than read a sign, so we built a game.

GO(A)LF is a wish and a putt in one. You write what you want on a slip of paper, seal it in a gatchapon capsule, and play the capsule down the golf course. Sink it and the wish counts. It stood in front of the Yeonsu-gu 2nd Office for one week, 15 to 21 October.

02 — MAKING

What we built
it out of.

Wood for the course, plastic crates for anything you sit on.

The drawing had two three-metre lanes inside the five by two metre space. We built one. We framed it in timber and laid artificial turf over it. The seats and the table are milk crates stacked and strapped together, with cardboard on top so there is a flat surface to write on.

03 — INSTALLATION DAY

Setting it up.

Four steps, floor to canopy.

We cleaned the ground and rolled the turf out first, then worked upward. The course down, the wish station up, plants along the edge so the space had an edge, canopy last.

04 — WHILE IT STOOD

One week
on the street.

Nobody needed it explained.

People found it on their own. Middle school students on the way home, office workers at lunch, parents with small kids. Hand someone a putter and they know what to do.

Four steps

  1. 01

    Write

    Write what you want on a slip at the wish station.

  2. 02

    Putt

  3. 03

  4. 04

    Share

What people did with it

Playing together

Friends, classmates and complete strangers taking turns.

Reading each other

People stood at the board, read what others wanted, then wrote their own.

Just sitting

Plenty of people never picked up a club. They sat in the shade.

Who stopped

It was not only students. Over that week the people who stopped were whoever happened to be walking past — including the staff of the office we had set up in front of.

Then the wind

Songdo is built on reclaimed land and the wind comes straight down the open boulevards. One day we came back and the sign was on its side, the crates had moved and the lane had come apart.

So we put it back. We re-laid the lane and the turf, hung tarpaulin walls on the canopy and tied a weight bag to every leg. It held for the rest of the week.

05 — WHAT WE LEARNED

What we got
out of it.

The people

The best part was watching it work. An empty space turned into somewhere strangers stood next to each other, wrote down something they wanted, and took a swing at it. People hoped out loud, together, on a strip of pavement everyone only ever walked past. Standing off to the side and seeing that happen was worth the whole semester.

Putting it back up

The wind taught us the other half. When we found it knocked over, the easy thing would have been to call it finished. We did not. We rebuilt it, and the second version was stronger than the first. Things fall over. You can put them back up.

What we got wrong

It was also our mistake. We designed for how it would look, not for the weather it had to stand in. Wind should have been in the drawings from the first sketch, and it was not. We only know that because we had to learn it on site, with the thing already on the ground.

If we did it again we would start with the wind and work backwards.
